# Teaching Note Protocol
**Version:** 2.0.0 (canonical 9-section structure, promoted from amodei-india-summit + boeing-737-max-grounding-decision-2019 sessions)
**Loaded by:** `:teach` command

---

## The Core Rule (Goldman)
"The teaching note is the document that unlocks the case. We facilitate case learning, we don't teach a case."

The TN must be at LEAST as long as the case narrative in word count. Shorter = insufficient depth. Enforce this before marking status TN COMPLETE.

---

## Structural Discipline (v2.0.0 — promoted from boeing-737-max-grounding-decision-2019)

The earlier protocol (v1.x) prescribed 13 mandatory sections including a Confidential Banner section, Pre-Class Assignment Questions, Theory Connections, Research Methodology Disclosure, and Supplementary Reading. The canonical implementation across `amodei-india-summit` and `boeing-737-max-grounding-decision-2019` settled at **9 sections**. The dropped sections were either redundant (Confidential Banner duplicates the navy banner already at top of page; Theory Connections duplicates Section 5's analytical framework) or rarely-used filler (Research Methodology, Supplementary Reading).

**Section heading format: `1. Section Title`** (period separator, NOT em-dash). The pattern `Section 1 — Title` violates the v10.23.0 em-dash discipline and reads as scaffolding-checklist rather than authored content.

**Section opener voice:** never lead with meta-explanation of what the section contains. Lead directly into the content. The pattern "This Teaching Note contains the case's epilogue, the four-option analytical framework, the suggested model answers, and the board plan" is the canonical AI-tell — promoted to known-corrections.md as a hard ban.

## TN Tone Discipline — The Facilitator's Voice

The TN must read as a facilitator's guide, not an advocate's brief. The instructor opens the classroom; the TN must not pre-close it.

### Banned patterns in model answers:
- "Strong students will recognize..." (prescriptive — implies weak students won't)
- "The correct analysis shows..." (there is no single correct analysis in a case)
- "The board was right about..." (moral verdict — let students argue this)
- "This was clearly..." or "obviously..." (forecloses debate)
- "The evidence proves..." (the TN presents interpretive possibilities, not proofs)
- "systematic dishonesty" or equivalent moral characterizations in the author's voice
- Phrases that pre-close the debate: "the most defensible answer combines...", "the only viable synthesis is..."

### Required patterns in model answers (promoted from amodei-india-summit + boeing v2.0.0):
- "Students may argue that..." (opens possibilities)
- "One reading of this evidence is..." (acknowledges alternatives)
- "Strong responses will surface both..." (rewards specific student moves rather than declaring correct answers)
- "The strongest version of this argument would note..." (steelmans without endorsing)
- "A counter-argument, drawing on [Framework], would hold..." (models genuine debate)
- "The instructor should let this counter-argument breathe" (direct facilitation guidance)
- "The case rewards students who name..." (criteria for evaluation, not conclusions)

### Moral neutrality for protagonists:
- Never describe a protagonist's behavior as "honest" or "dishonest" in the TN author's voice. Attribute such characterizations to named sources: "Toner described Altman's communications as 'outright lying' [S9]."
- Never describe organizational culture as "better" or "worse" — describe trade-offs with evidence
- The TN may present one interpretation as having stronger evidentiary support than another, but must always present the counter-interpretation and note what evidence would strengthen it

### The acid test:
If two instructors with opposing views on the case's central question could both use this TN to facilitate their preferred discussion direction, the tone is correct. If the TN forces one interpretation, it is overdetermined.

## Mandatory TN Sections (9 canonical)

Generate all 9 sections in the order listed below. Numbers are part of the heading; do not omit them. Do not insert a "Section 1 — Confidential Banner" or any other meta-section — the navy banner at the top of the rendered page handles confidentiality, and adding a prose section about it duplicates the warning.

---

### 1. Case Summary (~130 words, single paragraph)

Write one paragraph. Include: protagonist name, organization, decision date, the core dilemma, what students are asked to do, and one sentence at the end naming the analytical frame the case teaches (e.g., "framing the moment as a corporate-disclosure problem where..."). Do NOT reveal the outcome. Do NOT mention what actually happened.

The closing analytical-frame line is what distinguishes a good case summary from a plot summary. It tells the instructor in advance what the session will teach. If you cannot write that line, the case may not have a clear teaching point.

---

### 2. Learning Objectives (3-5 objectives + framing prose)

This section opens with a **framing paragraph** before the numbered objectives. The framing paragraph identifies: (a) the audience this case is appropriate for (MBA elective, executive ed, senior leader program); (b) what comparable canonical cases this case sits alongside; (c) any prerequisites or constraints; (d) a note on how the case ends (mid-decision Mode A means students argue from first principles, not retrospect). This framing paragraph is what distinguishes a teaching note from a syllabus entry.

After the framing prose, list 3-5 objectives in this format:

> "By the end of class discussion, students will be able to:"
> 1. **[Bloom's verb]** [object] (Bloom's level)
> 2. ...

Rules:
- Use exactly one Bloom's action verb per objective. No double-barreling ("analyze and evaluate").
- Must span at least 3 Bloom's levels.
- Bold the verb, plain text the rest, parenthetical Bloom's tag at end.

Bloom's verbs by level:
- **Remember:** identify, list, recall, name, recognize
- **Understand:** explain, summarize, describe, interpret, classify
- **Apply:** use, apply, demonstrate, implement, execute
- **Analyze:** compare, contrast, differentiate, examine, distinguish, diagnose
- **Evaluate:** assess, justify, defend, critique, recommend, judge
- **Create:** design, propose, formulate, construct, develop

---

### 3. Key Issues (3-6 tensions, BULLETED)

Format: bulleted list. Each bullet has a **bold lead phrase** stating the tension name, then 2-3 sentences explaining the tension and why it matters pedagogically. Each issue must correspond to at least one discussion question in Section 4.

Example:
> - **The defending-posture lock-in.** Public statements made to defend an aircraft become legal exhibits the moment a second crash occurs. The November 27 press release was load-bearing for everything Boeing said for the next four months; the SEC later cited that single statement as the foundation of a $200M investor-deception finding. The pedagogical question: when does a public statement become a structural commitment, and how should leaders treat statements that may need to be reversed?

The bullets-with-bold-lead format is mandatory. Headed paragraphs (used in v1.x) read as denser and harder to scan.

---

### 4. Discussion Questions with Suggested Answers (4-6 questions)

These questions are used DURING class discussion. Format for each:

> **Q1 — [Question text]** *(Bloom's level)*
>
> [Suggested answer: 150-300 words integrating at least one academic concept from the analytical framework. Do not just summarize the case — show the analytical move. Include "Strong responses will...", "Students may argue that...", "The instructor should let this breathe..." facilitator-voice patterns.]
>
> *Connect to scaffold:* This question executes Step [N] of the analytical scaffold (Section 5) — [explanation]. *Facilitation:* [technique].

Rules:
- Q1 at Understand or Apply. Q2-3 at Analyze. Q4-5 at Evaluate. Optional Q6 at Create.
- Bloom's tag at end of question line in italic small caps (e.g., *(Analyze)*).
- "Connect to scaffold:" goes inline at the END of each Q's answer in italic. NEVER as a separate section.
- "Facilitation:" suggests one technique per question (cold-call, think-pair-share, fishbowl, straw poll, individual writing, structured debate).
- Use em-dash in question header for question separation: "Q1 — [text]". This is the ONE place em-dashes are permitted in TN content (it's a heading-internal separator, not body prose).

Model answers MUST reference the primary framework. "This was a difficult trade-off" is not a model answer.

---

### 5. Analytical Framework (ONE primary + extension theories one-liner)

Single primary framework rule (promoted from amodei-india-summit, v1.1.0):

The TN names ONE primary framework, explains its constituent parts in 3-4 sentences with the strategic insight beyond intuition explicitly stated, then provides a **step-by-step decision scaffold** students can walk through.

Format:

> **Primary framework: [Framework name].**
>
> [3-4 sentence explanation of the framework, including the strategic insight beyond intuition.]
>
> ### Analytical Scaffold for Q[N] — Decision Walkthrough
>
> **Step 1 — [Step name].** [What students do at this step. Concrete instruction.]
>
> **Step 2 — [Step name].** [Step description.]
>
> **Step 3 — [Step name].** [Step description.]
>
> **Conclusion of the walkthrough:** [Summary of what the scaffold predicts, including the one criterion the scaffold does NOT capture — this is the bridge to the alternative-criterion discussion in Q3.]
>
> *Extensions for instructors who wish to deepen:* [List 3-4 extension theories as ONE single-line italic paragraph, each named with author and one-sentence connection. NOT as a separate "Theory Connections" section.]

Diagnostic test: "Could a student walk out and apply this framework to a different case tomorrow?" If they only learned 6+ named theories → TN failed. If they learned ONE usable mental model → TN succeeded.

The earlier "Theory Connections" section (v1.x) is now folded into this Section 5 as the extensions one-liner. Do not generate a separate Theory Connections section.

---

### 5a. Decision Options for Q[N] (Optional Discussion Aid)

The 3-5 options the protagonist faced, in Mode A-C cases. In Mode A this section appears ONLY in the TN — never in the case body.

Students must generate their own options during discussion BEFORE the instructor reveals this analysis. Do not distribute this section.

For each option:

> **Option [Letter] — [Descriptive Name].**
> What it entails: [1-2 sentences describing the specific action]
> *For:* [2-3 arguments with evidence from the case]
> *Against:* [2-3 arguments — genuine costs, sourced where possible]
> *Key assumption:* [What must be true for this option to succeed.]

After listing all options, include a comparative table with criteria like Short-term financial impact, Long-term financial impact, Stakeholder impact, Reversibility, Time to execute, Risk profile.

No option may be framed as obviously superior. AP1 and AP10 apply.

---

### 6. Board Plan (Three-Panel)

Cumulative board plan for the 75-90 minute class. Three panels, filled progressively.

**Panel 1 — FACTS** (Phase 1, early class)
Format: *Student says* "[X]" → *Board:* [exact board notation]

**Panel 2 — ANALYSIS** (Phase 2, mid-class)
Format: Framework name → Key insight → Board notation

**Panel 3 — RECOMMENDATIONS** (Phase 3, late class)
Format: *Student argues* "[position]" → *Board:* [conditional logic notation]

A completed board photograph should be usable as a one-page teaching summary.

---

### 7. Teaching Plan (75 or 90 minutes)

Build a session plan as a table:

> | Time | Duration | Activity | Discussion Focus | Facilitation |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | 0:00 | 5 min | Opening straw poll | [topic] | Anonymous vote |
> | 0:05 | 15 min | Phase 1 — [name] | [topic] | [technique] |
> | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

Rules:
- Opening must be an engaging activity (poll, cold call, fishbowl, surprising frame question). Never "Let's start by summarizing the case."
- After each phase, include a TRANSITION note in italics naming exactly what the instructor says.
- Reserve 10 minutes at the end for the epilogue reveal (Section 8).
- Never allocate more than 25% of total time to any single activity.

---

### 8. Epilogue — What Actually Happened

(The em-dash here is permitted because it's a heading separator, not body prose.)

Reveal at the END of class discussion — after students have committed to a position. Never distribute to students before class.

Format: chronological dated paragraphs from the decision moment forward. Each claim sourced.

> **[Date].** [What happened. Source citation in brackets.]

Add this note at the bottom: "*Reveal at the end of class discussion. Do not distribute before the final 10 minutes.*"

The epilogue should end with one summary line that ties the outcome back to the case's analytical frame.

---

### 9. Assignment Suggestions

Three follow-on assignments at varying levels of effort. Pick one based on the instructor's learning objective.

Format:

> **Short reflection (1-page, ~24 hours).** [What students do.] The constraint: [specifics]. The grade rubric rewards [criterion].
>
> **Comparative case memo (2,500 words, ~one week).** [What students do — usually compare the case against 2 canonical companion cases.] The deliverable is [format]: executive summary, framework application, recommendation.
>
> **[Custom track for senior audiences].** [Specialized assignment for board/exec participants.]

This section replaces the v1.x "Pre-Class Assignment Questions" section. Pre-class comprehension checks duplicate the case body itself; post-class assignments extend learning beyond the session and are far more valuable.

## Length Enforcement Rule

After generating all 9 sections:

1. Count words in the TN body (excluding HTML/CSS tags)
2. Count words in the case narrative (case body text only, excluding exhibits, source registry)
3. If TN word count < case narrative word count: expand in this order:
   - First: Expand model answers in Section 4 (add 50-100 words each, deepening the framework application)
   - Second: Expand the analytical scaffold in Section 5 (add a step or deepen step explanations)
   - Third: Add one more discussion question to Section 4
   - Fourth: Expand the framing paragraph in Section 2

Do not pad. Expand with substantive content only.

Target ratio: ≥1.5× case narrative for short cases (<3,000 words), ≥1.0× for standard cases (3,000-6,000 words), ≥0.7× for long cases (>6,000 words).

---

## A/B Case Pair TN Variant

When Case Structure = A+B Pair, modifications:

- **Section 8 (Epilogue)** is replaced by **"Case B Discussion Guide"** — analysis of the B case's outcomes, structured as a discussion framework.
- **Section 7 (Teaching Plan)** includes two sessions: Session 1 (Case A) and Session 2 (Case B reveal + comparative analysis).
- **Section 6 (Board Plan)** expands to 4 panels: FACTS | ANALYSIS | RECOMMENDATIONS | OUTCOMES (filled during Session 2).

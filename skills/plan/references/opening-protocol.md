# Opening Protocol
# Loaded by: atomic-case-writer during case drafting — apply before writing the first sentence
# Based on Goldman (Emerald EMCS EiC) and Craig (The Case Journal EiC) editorial standards

---

## The Mandatory Opening Structure

The first paragraph must contain all three elements within the first 3 sentences. Do not proceed to the background narrative until all three are established.

1. **PROTAGONIST** — named individual with full name, title, and organization
2. **DATE/LOCATION** — specific date (month and year minimum; day preferred when documented) and location if relevant
3. **DILEMMA** — the core tension or decision the protagonist faces, stated plainly

If you cannot establish all three within 3 sentences, your opening is not ready. Revise before continuing.

---

## The Goldman Rule

"Take us right to the action point, the decision point from the first few sentences. Open at the decision moment, then go BACK to tell the story."

Apply this structure rigidly:

- **Sentences 1–3:** The decision moment. Protagonist. Date. Dilemma.
- **Sentences 4–6:** Immediate stakes. Why this decision matters now. What the protagonist stands to gain or lose.
- **Then:** The narrative shifts backward to provide the context required to analyze the decision. This is where company background, market conditions, and precipitating events belong.

You are not writing a documentary that builds to the climax. You are opening at the climax and then providing the material students need to engage with it. If your opening reads like a corporate history or industry overview, you have started in the wrong place.

---

## The Craig Rule

"I saw a case where the first sentence went for four lines. Simplify."

Apply these constraints to the first sentence only:

- First sentence must be **under 25 words**
- No compound sentences in the first sentence
- No parenthetical clauses or em-dash asides in the first sentence
- Preferred structure: simple subject-verb-object. One clause. One idea.
- Commas are a warning sign. One comma in the first sentence is acceptable. Two is a red flag.

After the first sentence, normal prose complexity resumes. These constraints apply only to the opening sentence.

---

## Anti-Patterns — Never Open With These

Reject any opening that leads with one of the following. Move this content to the background section of the case.

- **Industry overview:** "The global [industry] market was valued at $X billion in [year]..."
- **Company history:** "Founded in [year], [Company] grew from a small startup to..."
- **Definition:** "[Term] refers to the practice of..."
- **Context dump before protagonist:** Any sentence where the protagonist is absent and context is being established before the human actor appears
- **Passive construction at the decision moment:** "A decision had to be made by [Name]..."
- **Outcome foreshadowing:** "What followed would become one of the most studied decisions in corporate history..."

If your opening paragraph does not name a person in the first sentence, rewrite it.

---

## Good Opening Examples

Study these for structure, not content. Each establishes protagonist, date, and dilemma within 2 sentences.

"On November 17, 2023, Satya Nadella, chief executive of Microsoft, learned that Sam Altman had been fired as CEO of OpenAI — one minute before the public announcement."

"In March 2019, WeWork's CEO Adam Neumann faced a decision that would define his legacy: whether to proceed with an IPO that analysts were already calling overvalued."

"Reed Hastings arrived at Netflix's Los Gatos headquarters on a Monday in September 2011 to find 800,000 customer cancellation notices in the company's inbox."

"On the morning of September 15, 2008, Richard Fuld, chairman and CEO of Lehman Brothers, watched the final negotiations for a buyer collapse — and with them, any remaining alternative to bankruptcy."

What each example does:
- Names a specific person with their title
- Anchors the moment with a specific date (not "in late 2023" or "that autumn")
- Places the protagonist at the threshold of a decision or consequence
- Uses simple sentence structure — no industry context, no company history
- Creates forward pull in the reader

---

## Validation Checklist

Run this checklist before advancing past the opening paragraph. Every item must be checked.

- [ ] Protagonist named with full name AND title in the first two sentences?
- [ ] Specific date (month and year minimum) appears by sentence 2?
- [ ] Core dilemma or decision is clear by the end of the opening paragraph?
- [ ] First sentence is under 25 words?
- [ ] All verbs in past tense?
- [ ] No hedging applied to the scenario-setting facts (date, location, title are factual and require no hedging)?
- [ ] No industry overview, company history, or definition in the opening paragraph?
- [ ] Protagonist is the grammatical subject of the first sentence or appears in the first sentence by name?

If any item is unchecked, revise the opening before continuing. Do not carry a failed opening into the background section.

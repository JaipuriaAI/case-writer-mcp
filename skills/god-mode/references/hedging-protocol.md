# Hedging Protocol
# Loaded by: atomic-case-writer during drafting and review phases
# These rules govern attribution language for all secondary-source cases.
# A secondary case describes what is publicly known — never what privately occurred.

---

## The Core Rule

You must never:
- Fabricate internal dialogue or private conversations
- Invent what a protagonist thought, felt, or considered
- Describe meetings, deliberations, or decisions that occurred privately without a documented public record
- Write "He decided..." without attributing that decision to a named public source

If you cannot attribute an internal state, a private action, or an unrecorded deliberation to a specific Tier 1–5 source, you must either rewrite it as observable behavior or remove it.

---

## Unattributed Internal States — Always Require Hedging or Rewriting

These constructions are forbidden without attribution. Apply the correction:

| Forbidden | Corrected |
|-----------|-----------|
| "Nadella was furious." | "According to [Source], Nadella was reportedly furious." OR rewrite as action: "Microsoft released a terse statement within hours." |
| "She considered three options." | "Public statements indicated the company was weighing several approaches." |
| "The board believed the valuation was excessive." | "In a statement, the board cited valuation concerns as a primary factor." |
| "He knew that the window was closing." | "Having reviewed the publicly disclosed market analysis, [Name] told [Publication] that urgency was paramount." |
| "They were confident the deal would close." | "Executives publicly expressed confidence that the deal would close by year-end." |
| "She regretted the decision." | Do not include — unless a named source documents the statement. |

The pattern: observable action and documented speech are always available. Private cognition never is.

## Standard Attribution Phrases

Use these phrases to anchor claims to their sources. Match the phrase to the source tier and your level of certainty.

**For actions confirmed by named sources:**
- "According to [Publication], ..."
- "As [Publication] reported in [Month Year], ..."
- "[Name] stated in a [press conference / earnings call / letter to shareholders] that ..."
- "In a [Month Year] interview with [Publication], [Name] said ..."
- "The company disclosed in its [Year] annual report that ..."

**For inferred but unverified actions (use sparingly):**
- "Reports suggested ..."
- "Industry observers noted ..."
- "Based on publicly available information ..."
- "Available records indicate ..."

**For documented information gaps:**
- "Publicly available information does not reveal the internal deliberations behind this decision."
- "The internal discussions surrounding this decision were not made public."
- "No official statement addressed [specific topic]."
- "The company declined to comment on [specific claim], and no independent account has emerged."

Never use "reportedly" as a verbal shrug to insert unverified claims. "Reportedly" is reserved for cases where a reputable Tier 2 source uses that qualifier itself, or where you have indirect but credible sourcing.

---

## What Can Be Stated as Fact — No Hedging Needed

These categories require no hedging language. State them directly.

- Financial figures drawn from Tier 1 sources (SEC filings, audited accounts, regulatory orders)
- Direct quotes with a named speaker, named publication, and specific date
- Official announcements published via press release or regulatory filing
- Stock price movements and trading volumes (verifiable from public market data)
- Named board votes, shareholder votes, or governance actions recorded in official minutes or filings
- Court decisions, regulatory rulings, and legislative outcomes documented in public records

---

## Protagonist Voice Construction

For secondary cases, you must build the protagonist's voice exclusively from documented public sources. Work down this hierarchy in order:

1. Annual report letters to shareholders — richest source of strategic voice and self-assessment
2. Earnings call transcripts — most candid; executives speak at length and on the record
3. Named press interviews — cite the publication and date in the attribution
4. Conference keynotes and public speeches — often publicly recorded and transcribed
5. Social media posts — acceptable for tech executives and public figures; cite the platform and post date

Never: attribute internal thoughts, private statements, off-the-record conversations, or invented reasoning to the protagonist. If a protagonist's voice is thin in the public record, say so in the case: "Few public statements from [Name] addressed this specific decision."

---

## Living Individuals — Extra Care

When writing about living individuals, apply these additional constraints:

- Use published quotes only — never paraphrase from memory or general knowledge
- Verify every quote against the original source, not a secondary citation of a quote
- Do not characterize personality traits, leadership style, or character beyond what the documented record shows
- Never include references to mental health, personal relationships, private life, or family matters unless those are themselves central to a documented public controversy and sourced from Tier 1–2
- Do not describe physical appearance, mannerisms, or personal habits unless documented in a named, credible source
- If a quote has been disputed or retracted, note the dispute — do not present it as settled

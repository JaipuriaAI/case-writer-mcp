# Case Type Taxonomy

## Selection Flowchart

Run through these questions in order. Stop at the first match.

1. Is the material about a SINGLE INCIDENT (≤2 days) with an ethical or crisis dimension? → **Critical Incident** (2-4 pages)
2. Does the protagonist face a CLEAR STRATEGIC FORK with 3+ distinct options and identifiable trade-offs? → **Decision Case** (8-15 pages)
3. Is the material condensed enough for ONE SESSION and has ONE focused decision? → **Mini-Case / Caselet** (1-3 pages)
4. Does the story NATURALLY SEQUENCE into distinct decision → outcome → consequence phases? → **Series A/B/C** (4-8 pages each)
5. Is the material designed to DEMONSTRATE ONE SPECIFIC FRAMEWORK with limited ambiguity? → **Illustrative** (4-8 pages)
6. Does the data present multiple LEGITIMATE INTERPRETATIONS with no clear right answer for a senior audience? → **Exploratory** (10-18 pages)
7. Is the timeline LONG (multiple years) with MULTIPLE STAKEHOLDERS and evolving dynamics? → **Descriptive** (12-20 pages)
8. Is the focus on comparing MULTIPLE COMPANIES or INDUSTRY-LEVEL dynamics? → **Industry Note** (8-15 pages)

## Full Case Type Reference

### 1. Decision Case

**Description:** The canonical Harvard case format. A protagonist at a genuine decision fork must choose among 3-5 options before a real deadline. The case ends at the moment of choice — outcomes are never revealed in the case body.

**Narrative length:** 8-15 pages + exhibits

**Section structure:**
- Hook opening (½ page — protagonist at decision moment)
- Protagonist and context (1-2 pages)
- Industry and competitive context (2-3 pages)
- Company history and strategy (2-3 pages)
- The situation or crisis (2-3 pages)
- Decision moment (1/2-1 page — ending per selected mode)

**Exhibit count:** 3-6

**Decision point:** Yes — specific, time-bound, 3-5 options with genuine trade-offs. Ending mode selected during :plan Case Architecture Gate.

**Teaching note ratio:** TN must be ≥1.0x the case narrative length

**Best for:** MBA strategy, corporate governance, leadership, organizational behavior

**What NOT to use it for:** Situations where the outcome is already universally known — use Descriptive instead, as hindsight bias destroys the deliberation

**Key structural constraint:** The case ENDS at the decision point. Outcomes, consequences, and what actually happened go in the teaching note epilogue only — never in the case body. Default ending: Mode A (Interrupted Narrative). Structured fork (Mode D) available for scaffolded contexts.

### 2. Critical Incident

**Description:** A compressed case built around a single high-stakes incident — typically ethical, interpersonal, or crisis-driven. Brevity is structural: the incident must be rendered in fine-grained real-time detail, not summarized.

**Narrative length:** 2-4 pages + exhibits

**Section structure:**
- Scene-setting (1 paragraph — place, time, who is present)
- Build-up (½ page — what led to this moment)
- The incident in real-time detail (1-2 pages — no summarizing)
- Immediate aftermath (½ page)
- Immediate aftermath and temporal freeze (½ page)

**Exhibit count:** 0-2 (correspondence, statements — not financial)

**Decision point:** Yes — ethically charged, immediate, often personal

**Teaching note ratio:** TN must be ≥1.5x the case length. The brief case requires disproportionately extensive analysis in the teaching note.

**Best for:** Ethics, crisis management, negotiation, DEI, leadership

**What NOT to use it for:** Complex multi-stakeholder strategic situations — the format cannot carry multiple perspectives without losing its punch

**Key structural constraint:** Every sentence of the incident section is load-bearing. No padding, no background filler. If a sentence can be removed without losing meaning, remove it.

---

### 3. Illustrative

**Description:** A teaching case built to demonstrate how a specific framework applies to a real situation. Unlike the Decision Case, it may reveal what actually happened — the goal is framework application, not deliberation under uncertainty.

**Narrative length:** 4-8 pages

**Section structure:**
- Concise situation (1-2 pages)
- Challenge framed within framework logic (1-2 pages — implicit, never name the framework)
- Evidence aligned to framework dimensions (1-2 pages)
- What happened and why (1-2 pages — outcomes CAN be revealed)

**Exhibit count:** 2-4

**Decision point:** No — analytical question instead (e.g., "How does this situation illustrate the dynamics of two-sided markets?")

**Teaching note ratio:** TN must be ≥1.0x

**Best for:** Introductory courses, framework teaching, executive education where participants need scaffolding

**What NOT to use it for:** Advanced audiences who need ambiguity — the controlled framing feels condescending at Level 4+

**Key structural constraint:** The framework must be IMPLICIT in the case body — never name it. Name and explain it in the teaching note. The pedagogical task for students is recognizing which framework applies.

---

### 4. Descriptive

**Description:** A long-form case that maps the evolution of a company, organization, or situation over time. The purpose is analysis and understanding, not decision-making. Stakeholders, dynamics, and forces are the subject.

**Narrative length:** 12-20 pages

**Section structure:**
- Current state (1-2 pages — where things stand now)
- Historical genesis (3-4 pages — how it came to be)
- Market dynamics (2-3 pages)
- Organizational structure and culture (2-3 pages)
- Stakeholder positions (2-3 pages)
- Where things stand now (1-2 pages — analytical setup, not a decision)

**Exhibit count:** 4-8 (org charts, timelines, market data)

**Decision point:** No — analytical question about evolution or dynamics (e.g., "Why did this organization develop in this direction?")

**Teaching note ratio:** TN must be ≥1.0x

**Best for:** Industry analysis, organizational evolution, path dependency, international business

**What NOT to use it for:** Time-constrained sessions — 12-20 pages is too dense for a 75-minute class discussion

**Key structural constraint:** Every historical section must connect explicitly to the current situation. Historical background that does not illuminate the present state must be cut.

---

### 5. Exploratory

**Description:** A case built around genuine ambiguity — not just about what to do, but what the problem actually is. Multiple data sources conflict. Stakeholder perspectives diverge. The case does not resolve the ambiguity; that is the point.

**Narrative length:** 10-18 pages

**Section structure:**
- Ambiguous opening situation (1-2 pages)
- Multiple data sources with conflicting implications (3-4 pages)
- Conflicting stakeholder perspectives (3-4 pages — present each perspective fairly)
- Minimal interpretation (2-3 pages — context only, no editorial framing)
- Data exhibits (presented without guidance on which to trust)

**Exhibit count:** 3-6 (conflicting data preferred — two exhibits that contradict each other serve the format)

**Decision point:** Ambiguous — the first task is diagnosing WHAT the problem is, not how to solve it

**Teaching note ratio:** TN must be ≥1.0x

**Best for:** Advanced MBA, consulting skills courses, research methods, problem-finding workshops

**What NOT to use it for:** Students who need structured guidance — the lack of resolution creates anxiety in early-stage learners

**Key structural constraint:** Do NOT resolve the ambiguity in the case body. If you find yourself explaining what the data "really" means, you are writing a Decision Case, not an Exploratory.

---

### 6. Mini-Case / Caselet

**Description:** A compressed single-session case designed for in-class reading and immediate discussion. One protagonist, one decision, one session. The format prioritizes accessibility and speed over analytical depth.

**Narrative length:** 1-3 pages

**Section structure:**
- Setup (½ page — context and protagonist)
- Situation (½ page — the challenge)
- Decision point (½ page — per ending mode; Mode D recommended for Level 1)

**Exhibit count:** 1-2

**Decision point:** Yes — condensed but specific, with a clear deadline

**Teaching note ratio:** TN must be ≥2.0x the case length. The brevity of the case requires substantial teaching note support to generate a full discussion.

**Best for:** Workshops, executive education, online asynchronous learning, in-class warm-up exercises, programs where pre-reading cannot be guaranteed

**What NOT to use it for:** Deep analytical discussions requiring 90 minutes — the limited data does not support extended examination

**Key structural constraint:** Students must be able to read the case in class in under 15 minutes. If the narrative exceeds this, cut it.

---

### 7. Series Cases (A/B/C)

**Description:** A multi-part case sequence where each part is released and taught sequentially. Part A presents a decision, Part B reveals early outcomes and a new decision, Part C reveals long-term consequences and reflections. Each part is a self-contained case.

**Narrative length:**
- Part A: 4-8 pages (the decision)
- Part B: 6-10 pages (implementation and early outcomes)
- Part C: 6-10 pages (long-term consequences and reflections)

**Section structure (per part):**
- Part A: Hook → context → decision fork
- Part B: Decision made → implementation challenges → new decision fork
- Part C: Long-term outcomes → stakeholder consequences → reflection questions

**Exhibit count:** 2-4 per part

**Decision point:** Sequential — Part A ends at the first decision, Part B reveals early outcomes and presents a second decision, Part C reveals long-term consequences

**Teaching note ratio:** TN must be ≥1.0x per part (three separate teaching notes)

**Best for:** Change management, strategy implementation, multi-session MBA programs, executive education that spans multiple days

**What NOT to use it for:** Single-session use — the format requires sequential distribution and loses its function if collapsed

**Key structural constraint:** Parts B and C must not be distributed until Part A has been taught. Distribute only what students are ready to discuss.

---

### 8. Industry Note

**Description:** A reference document that describes an industry rather than a company. No protagonist. Used as prerequisite context before a company-level case session or as standalone industry analysis.

**Narrative length:** 8-15 pages

**Section structure:**
- Industry definition and scope (1-2 pages)
- Historical evolution (2-3 pages)
- Market structure (1-2 pages)
- Competitive dynamics (2-3 pages)
- Key players compared (1-2 pages)
- Regulatory and macro environment (1-2 pages)
- Outlook (1 page)

**Exhibit count:** 4-8 (comparison tables, market sizing data, growth charts)

**Decision point:** None — the analytical question concerns the industry structure, not a protagonist's choice

**Teaching note ratio:** TN must be ≥0.5x (often used as pre-reading, not a discussion anchor)

**Best for:** Industry analysis courses, setting context for a company case session, background reading in executive programs

**What NOT to use it for:** Standalone discussion without an accompanying company case — without a protagonist, student engagement drops sharply

**Key structural constraint:** There is no protagonist. The industry itself is the subject. Remove any sentence that centers a single company's perspective as the primary frame.

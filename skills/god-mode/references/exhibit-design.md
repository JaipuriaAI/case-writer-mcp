# Exhibit Design

## Types of Exhibits and When to Use Each

Select exhibit type based on what the decision requires — not on what data is available. Only include exhibits that a student must engage with to make or analyze the decision.

1. **Financial statements** — Income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement: use when financial position, profitability, or liquidity is material to the decision
2. **Comparative tables** — Competitor benchmarking, option matrices: use when the decision requires comparing multiple companies or multiple options across consistent metrics
3. **Market data tables** — Market size, growth rates, share data by segment: use when industry context or market trajectory is material to evaluating the options
4. **Timeline / chronology** — Key events in sequential order: use when temporal sequence is required to understand how the situation developed
5. **Organizational charts** — Reporting relationships, governance structure, board composition: use when org structure or authority is relevant to the decision
6. **Correspondence** — Letters, emails, official statements drawn from public sources: use in critical incident and ethical cases where the tone and language of communication are part of the case material
7. **Process diagrams** — Value chains, operational workflows, supply chain maps: use in operational cases where the decision is about how work flows, not just what to decide

## Mandatory Rules (Non-Negotiable)

Apply these rules to every exhibit in every case. There are no exceptions.

**1. Number sequentially in order of first reference.** Exhibit 1 must be referenced in the narrative before Exhibit 2. Never assign exhibit numbers out of order. If you assign a number and later move the reference, renumber.

**2. Every exhibit has a source line.** Every exhibit must end with a source attribution in italics:
- *Source: Company 10-K Filing, FY2023*
- *Source: Author compilation from Bloomberg Terminal, company annual reports 2019-2023*
- *Source: [URL], archived via Wayback Machine, [date]*
If you cannot attribute the data, do not include it.

**3. Every exhibit is referenced in the narrative before it appears.** Use one of these forms:
- "...as shown in Exhibit 1"
- "...(see Exhibit 2)"
- "Exhibit 3 shows the breakdown by region."
The reference must appear in the case body before the exhibit page. Do not let any exhibit appear unreferenced.

**4. No orphaned exhibits.** If an exhibit is not referenced in the narrative, remove it. An exhibit students must discover on their own is not an exhibit — it is a data dump.

**5. Every exhibit has a descriptive title that identifies what it shows and the time period.** Correct: "Exhibit 2: WeWork Revenue and Net Loss, 2016–2019 (USD millions)". Incorrect: "Exhibit 2: Financial Data". The title must tell a reader exactly what they are looking at before they read a single cell.

## Design for Analysis (Not Just Information)

Exhibits that merely present information are insufficient. Every exhibit must enable student calculation, comparison, or inference. Apply these principles:

**Financial exhibits:** Include raw numbers alongside any ratios. Students must be able to calculate the ratios themselves — presenting only pre-calculated percentages removes the analytical task. Include at least 3 years of data so students can assess trends.

**Comparison tables:** Use identical metrics across all companies or options so students can rank and compare consistently. If one row is missing data for a company, mark it explicitly as "N/A" or "—" rather than leaving it blank (blank implies error or omission; "—" signals the data does not exist or was not disclosed).

**Market data:** Include a minimum of 3 years so students can identify growth rates, inflection points, and trajectory. A single year of market data is a fact, not an analytical resource.

**Units always specified in column headers:** Write "Revenue (USD millions)" not "Revenue." Write "Market Share (%)" not "Market Share." Never make a student guess the unit.

**Temporal consistency:** All exhibits in the same case use the same currency and the same time period conventions. Do not mix calendar year and fiscal year data without a footnote explaining the difference.

## Formatting Rules

Apply these to every table and exhibit.

**Column headers:** Must identify the metric AND the unit. "Revenue (USD millions)" and "EBITDA Margin (%)" are correct. "Revenue" and "Margin" are not.

**Number formatting:** Choose one convention and apply it consistently throughout all exhibits in the case. Either use commas for thousands throughout (1,234) or do not (1234). Never mix.

**Missing data:** Use "—" (em dash) for data that genuinely does not exist or was not disclosed. Use "N/A" for data that is not applicable to a particular row. Never leave cells blank — blank cells imply that data exists but was accidentally omitted.

**Source line:** Place in italics at the bottom of each exhibit, outside the table border:
*Source: [attribution]*

**Exhibit title:** Format as bold, placed above the exhibit:
**EXHIBIT [N] — [DESCRIPTIVE TITLE IN ALL CAPS]**

Example: **EXHIBIT 3 — OPENAI REVENUE AND HEADCOUNT, 2020–2023**

## For Internet-Sourced Data

When compiling data from multiple public sources into a single exhibit, document your methodology. Students and faculty must be able to verify your sources.

**Mixed-source exhibits:** Add a methodology footnote below the source line:
*Note: Revenue figures compiled from annual reports, 10-K filings, and Bloomberg data, 2019–2023. Fiscal year end dates vary by company: Apple (September), Microsoft (June), Alphabet (December).*

**Currency conversions:** Document the exchange rate and source:
*All non-USD figures converted at average annual exchange rate. Source: Federal Reserve H.10 Statistical Release.*

**Historical web data:** If you accessed a page via archive:
*Source: [URL] via Wayback Machine, archived [exact date, e.g., March 14, 2023].*

**Estimates and projections:** Mark explicitly inline within the table:
- Estimated figures: append "(E)" to the cell value — e.g., "$4.2B (E)"
- Projected figures: append "(P)" — e.g., "$6.1B (P)"
- Add a footnote explaining the estimation methodology.

## Exhibit Placement

**All exhibits appear after the full narrative.** Do not interrupt the case body with exhibits — place them in sequence after the final paragraph of the narrative (after the temporal freeze or decision point).

**Each exhibit on its own page.** Use a page break before each exhibit. Do not run two exhibits on the same page.

**Source registry appears after all exhibits.** On a dedicated final page titled "Sources," list all sources cited across exhibits in alphabetical order by author or organization. This is separate from the inline source attributions on each exhibit — the registry provides a complete bibliography for faculty verification.
